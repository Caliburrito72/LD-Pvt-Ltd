The human problem

A simple meal can become a daily dependency.

The concept is built around reducing the amount of direct assistance required for routine feeding while keeping the user at the centre of control.

Caregiver dependence

Everyday feeding can require another person to assist with basic movements.

Hand coordination

Limited fine motor control can make conventional utensils difficult to operate.

Everyday vessels

A practical assistive system should work with ordinary bowls, plates and cups.

Social acceptability

The technology should fit naturally into everyday living rather than add another barrier.

The product

One arm. Two control modes. An adaptive grip.

The current concept connects a robotic arm, soft pneumatic gripping, computer vision and intuitive user control into one feeding platform.

01

Foot joystick + vision

For users who can operate a foot interface.

Base rotation, elbow movement and spoon-neck tilt.
Camera tracks the user's mouth position.
Face-loss detection stops the system.
02

Vision only

For users who cannot use the foot interface.

Mouth position is estimated through landmark detection.
Feeding sequence: position → mouth open → bite trigger.
The robot stops when the face is lost.
How the system works

The technical pipeline is designed to read the user's intent, plan a motion and execute it with safety layers around the physical robot.

01
See

Camera observes food, utensil and face.

02
Understand

Vision models identify useful objects and mouth pose.

03
Plan

Motion planning converts perception into a feeding path.

04
Move

Arm actuators execute the planned movement.

05
Feed

The utensil approaches the mouth and responds to the trigger.

06
Protect

Face-loss, E-stop and speed controls remain in the loop.

Feasibility & responsibility

Assistive intelligence needs responsible engineering.

The source material identifies current engineering challenges and corresponding mitigation directions. These are presented here as the project's working approach, not as finished certifications.

01

Soft-base fatigue

Seam leakage is approached with one-piece casting, leak testing and interchangeable fingers.

02

Vision limits

Current vision operates on a controlled camera setup; depth sensing is a future direction.

03

Unexpected motion

E-stop, speed limitation and layered control logic help reduce physical risk.

04

Regulatory path

CDSCO / MDR 2017, ISO 13485, IEC 60601, FDA and EU MDR are identified as considerations.
